# Assumptions register

Rule: nothing the game does is left out of a model or a design because it is inconvenient. A
mechanic is either modelled from the game's code and checked against a live run, or it is listed
here with why it is unresolved, why the current treatment is defensible, what breaks if it is wrong,
and how to settle it. "Not modelled" with no entry here is a defect.

Status: **RESOLVED** (measured, entry kept for the record), **BOUNDED** (unresolved but the error has
a known ceiling), **OPEN** (unresolved, could change a conclusion).

## The simulator (`tools/Balance`)

| # | Assumption or simplification | Status | Justification | If wrong | How to settle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| S1 | Tables dumped from the game are interpolated linearly, and `planet.py` is the same arithmetic as `Climate.cs` | RESOLVED | The game evaluated every table. Live: Mars day 281.29 K vs 281.5 model, night 226.24 vs 226.31; Europa 133.05 vs 133.07 and 124.0 vs 124.0; with the mod's rule active, Venus 322.17 vs 322.3 and Vulcan within 0.19 K at six sun angles through a day. **MEASURED**: between knots the error is what the knot spacing makes it. Five-degree samples of Vulcan's base curve were 5 K out where it bends at dusk, so `-Dump` now samples that curve every degree and the whole storm run holds within 0.35 K | Under 0.3 K, times the anchor gain on hot worlds | `run.ps1 -Observe` on any world |
| S2 | A mix is judged in its settled state: weather offset, latent heat and external heat are zero | BOUNDED | Weather is an event, and its offset now shrinks with the planet (**MEASURED** live on Vulcan, cooled and untouched: DEFECTS.md D17). A storm is still the harshest hour a planet has: the cooled Vulcan lost its CO2 and pollutant to the ice clouds while the ash storm ran. External heat fades by design. Latent heat does NOT return to zero by itself: the game books it unevenly (**MEASURED**: 17 % stays after a freeze-and-return), so the mod fades and bounds it like external heat (D14). **MEASURED** in play: an ordinary sealed room vented back outdoors was worth 0.13 to 0.18 K of external heat, which then faded | A planet that has just cycled a lot of gas through its caps reads a few kelvin off the model for an hour or two | Watch the latent counter fade in a live run |
| S3 | Hottest and coldest are taken over every sun angle 0-180 and both ends of the orbit | BOUNDED | A base is at one latitude and sees a narrower range (**MEASURED** Mars 38-166 degrees). Taking the full range makes a recipe that passes habitable anywhere, any season | Recipes cost slightly more than a given base site needs | Dump the per-site angle range if tighter numbers are ever wanted |
| S4 | Phase change is a settled-state rule: a gas below freezing point + 2 K at the coldest hour is not in the air; a gas below its boiling point + 2 K at the planet's total pressure (once that pressure reaches the gas's minimum liquid pressure) rains out | RESOLVED | **CODE**: `FreezeGlobalGasToIceClouds` passes `MoleQuantity.MaxValue`, the whole gas in one tick. **MEASURED**: 20 mol per cell of CO2 added to Europa at 134 K was out of the air within five ticks, snow was scheduled, and it moved to the ice caps at the game's 3000 mol per tick. **MEASURED** on a Mars held at 216 K by night, 292 K by day (TEMPERATURE.md): a gas sitting on its threshold does not linger or oscillate part-way. All 5.5 mol per cell went at dusk in a single tick and came back after dawn at exactly the 10 % per tick the code says, leaving 0.8 % a night in the ice caps, which melts back by day. The settled-state rule is therefore the right call for a recipe: such a mix has no air at night | A recipe judged unstable is unstable for a reason: its air is gone every night, not thinned | - |
| S5 | Gas that froze or rained out returns when the planet is warm enough (`path.py`, `_comes_back`) | RESOLVED | **MEASURED**: CO2 frozen out on Europa came straight back from tank liquid when the planet was warmed early, and from the ice caps at the game's own 1,000 mol per tick when warmed late (TEMPERATURE.md). It needed a fix: the game evaluates the caps' temperature on the caps, which hold no gas | Return from the caps is slow: 20 mol per cell takes about 40 minutes at Standard size and 14 hours at Shipped | - |
| S6 | Liquid toxins lying outdoors do not poison through breathing | RESOLVED | **CODE**: `Atmosphere.PartialPressureHumanToxins` (`Atmosphere.cs:812-823`) sums the gas species only (pollutant, volatiles, hydrazine, silanol, HCl). `GasMixture.TotalToxins`, which includes liquids, is read only by water bottle fillers and fountains. Whether standing in a liquid hurts some other way was not traced | A rained-out toxin that is still harmful on contact | Stand a test character in it |
| S7 | The planet is one well-mixed box; gas in outdoor cells in transit is ignored | BOUNDED | **MEASURED**: 100,000 mol injected spreads and drains into the planet in about 70 ticks. **MEASURED** in play: 7,207 mol drawn out of the planet and released came back within about two minutes, leaving 34.6 mol, about 3.8 cells, still in the real outdoor cells by the door. Cells drain back to the tank; they do not stand at planet density | None at planet scale | - |
| S8 | Fuel and an oxidiser are never outdoors together in a recommended path | RESOLVED | **CODE** (`Atmosphere.TryCombust`): a sparked cell burns any fuel beside any oxidiser, with no minimum ratio or temperature, and sparks its neighbours; above 573 K no spark is needed. `path.py` forbids the combination outright (`SAFE = True`) and has to take Europa's oxygen out of the way before using volatiles | A player who accepts the fire risk has a cheaper route; `SAFE = False` prices it | - |
| S9 | Adding and removing are priced apart; removal through vents is dilution | RESOLVED as a design choice | One rate for both was wrong: it priced a mole removed as though a rocket had to mine it, and Venus's route is 85 % removal. `cost.py` prices addition in ice mined and removal by integrating dn/dt = -intake x n/N over the route's own timeline, with the game's vent rule (**CODE** `ActiveVent.PumpGasToPipe`). The result agrees with `removal.py`'s independent sum: Venus 12 h against 11, Vulcan 13 against 11 | Nothing; this entry is the fix | - |
| S12 | Somewhere to put what is removed | BOUNDED | Filtration is not a limit (**CODE**): a filter wears by ticks in use, not by moles (`GasFilter`: a large filter lasts 40 hours of use whatever flows), and a Filtration unit outruns the vents. Storage is a build cost, not a rate: a big tank (50,000 L, **MEASURED**) holds about 1.2 M mol of gas at the 60.8 MPa pipe limit, or as liquid 1.25 M mol of CO2 (0.04 L/mol) or 1.8 M mol of HCl (0.028 L/mol, **CODE** `Chemistry.MOLAR_VOLUME_*`). Venus at Standard size is about 48 big tanks, Europa 67. There is no bulk sink: gas released above the 1,000 m space line is given to the planet, not destroyed (`Atmosphere.LerpToGlobalAtmosphere`); the exceptions are rocket exhaust above that line and the small read-only-copy leaks listed in INTERACTIONS.md. Trader sales are now priced (S16) and are too small to change this on any world but Vulcan | Tank count is the real cost of a removal world; unplayed | Play it |
| S10 | What a base runs: ice mined (small 13k, medium 68k, mega 328k mol/h) and inward vents (4, 8, 20) | OPEN | Built from measured machine rates but assumed duty cycles, and the vent counts are assumed outright. Four active vents is 400 W and twenty large powered is 10 kW, which is in proportion to the rest of each tier. Gas traders, composters and in-place CO2 to O2 conversion are left out of both capacities, so the tiers are a lower bound | The hour labels on the presets move. The vent count also decides whether a world reads as addition-bound: Venus flips below 6 large powered vents, Mimas and Europa below 4, Vulcan below 1. `cost.py` prints that figure per world so the verdict can be checked | ROADMAP item 4; play a removal world and count the vents actually built |
| S11 | `path.py` is a greedy planner | BOUNDED | "No path found" is not proof of none; a found path's cost is an upper bound | Costs overstated | Replay found paths live at their waypoints |
| S13 | A mole of gas put outdoors costs 24/22 moles of ice mined | BOUNDED | **CODE** `MineableDeposit.SpawnIce`: a mined ice item carries the deposit's own mixture, and the shipped compositions in `rocketlocations.xml` are 24 moles an item. The richest site for oxygen, nitrogen, volatiles or helium gives 22 of those 24; for carbon dioxide only 14, which is why burning volatiles for it is cheaper. The other 2 moles are counted as waste even where they are a gas the recipe also wants (an oxygen site carries 2 carbon dioxide). Whether a rich site is within reach on a given save is not modelled | Addition hours out by under 10 % | Survey a real save and record the sites it offers |
| S14 | Removals that overlap in the route share one stream of air; removals that do not are charged separately | BOUNDED | One filtration line can hold filters for several gases, so a single stream takes them all out in proportion and the cost is the deepest cut rather than the sum. `cost.py` groups gases whose removal stretches overlap in the plan. Where `path.py` serialises two removals that could have run together (Mimas takes its helium out and then its volatiles) the model charges both | Removal hours overstated on Mimas and Vulcan, where removal is not the binding phase anyway | Group by what is in the air rather than by plan order |
| S15 | Burners, crushers and filtration keep up with the ice arriving | OPEN | Nothing prices the machines between the ice and the air. An ice crusher is 1 item per 100 ms, far more than four rockets deliver, but the burn that makes carbon dioxide is not priced at all: there is no measured burn rate for a furnace or a combustion centrifuge in hand. Mars needs 22 moles per cell of volatiles burnt, 5.6 M mol at Standard size | Addition hours understated on every world that burns for its carbon dioxide, which is all of them but Venus | Measure a furnace's moles an hour in a live run |
| S16 | The gas trader's buy orders are a real but small sink | BOUNDED | **CODE**: `tradeables.xml` ships a "World Atmosphere" block buying Venus carbon dioxide, Vulcan volatiles and hydrogen, Europa oxygen and Mars carbon dioxide. A unit is 100 moles, an order is 50-200 units, doubled for a large trader (`TraderDataInstance.ApplyBulkMultiplier`). The visit cycle is taken as the LargeTrader slot's own LifeTime of 2400 s plus its down time and contact time, about 50 minutes, and every visit is ASSUMED to be a gas trader bought out. That is the ceiling, not the expectation: trader type is a random pick per slot. It gives 18,000 mol/h on Venus, 2,400 h to clear its carbon dioxide against a 40 h project, and on Vulcan 190 h to clear the volatiles against a 222 h project | Only Vulcan changes, and only towards fewer tanks | Play Vulcan and count the visits |

## The mod's temperature rule (`src/Patching/Climate.cs`)

These are design choices, not facts about the game. Each is the same for every world.

| # | Choice | Status | Justification | If wrong | Tunable by a player? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| M1 | The default greenhouse and density curves are Mars's | chosen | The one world the developers tuned | Response too strong or weak elsewhere | Yes: curves file, and a strength setting each |
| M2 | A hot world's shipped mean is bare-rock equilibrium plus warming from its starting air, and the positive part of the greenhouse response is scaled to match | chosen | Without it Venus holds about 440 K the player can never remove. Physically: that is what a greenhouse is | Hot worlds too easy or too hard | Through the curves file, which reshapes the route. `GhgResponseScale` is divided out of the gain so that no value of it can make a hot world impossible |
| M3 | Anchoring blends in from a starting greenhouse index of 5 to 10, gain capped at 10 | chosen | No shipped world sits between 5 and 10 (Venus 33, Vulcan 18.5, Europa -8); the blend removes a cliff for custom worlds, the cap bounds a curve with g(start) near zero | A custom world in the band behaves between the two rules | No |
| M4 | Anchor albedo fixed at 0.3, separate from the `AirlessAlbedo` setting | chosen | The setting is for airless worlds; letting it move a touched Venus by 30 K mid-save would be a trap | Equilibrium off by about 10 K per 0.1 albedo | No |
| M5 | Swing damping is the density curve's day-night gap relative to the gap at the starting air, clamped 0 to 1 | chosen | Same physics as the game's Mars density curve, made proportional so it works on a world with a 766 K swing. Clamped because thinning the air past the start would otherwise multiply the swing (Vulcan vacuum: x3.9, nights to 0 K) | Thinning a world's air never makes nights colder than shipped | `DensityResponseScale`, an exponent: 0 off, more bites sooner, end point unchanged |
| M6 | Cooling (negative greenhouse response) is never amplified by the anchor gain | chosen | Otherwise flooding Vulcan with nitrogen fixes it with no removal at all | - | No |
| M7 | An airless world with no base curve gets grey-body equilibrium, albedo 0.3, +50 / -60 K day and night | chosen | The shipped game reads 0 K there. 278.6 K at 1367 W/m2 is textbook | Moon and Mimas start points shift | `AirlessAlbedo` |
| M8 | External heat fades with a 60 minute half-life and is capped at 50 K | chosen, untuned | The game never drains it (defect D3). **MEASURED** in play: a 7,436 L room at 636 kPa vented outdoors was worth 0.18 K, so the 50 K cap is nowhere near at base scale | Heating the planet with machines is too weak or too strong a tool | Both are settings |
| M9 | The postfix recomputes the greenhouse index the game just computed | BOUNDED | Exact and simple; about ten curve evaluations per call on top of the game's own | Frame time in a base with thousands of open outdoor cells | Measure in ROADMAP item 6 |
| M10 | Harmony keeps the patched temperature method from being inlined | RESOLVED: every `-Model` run shows the adjustment applied | The method is long (not an inlining candidate) and is patched at load | The response silently does nothing | `terraform` shows "adding N K now"; LiveCheck compares game to model |

## The trace gas rule (`src/Patching/TraceGases.cs`)

| # | Choice or assumption | Status | Justification | If wrong | Tunable by a player? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T0 | Trace gas gathering ships off | chosen (owner, 2026-09-26) | Seen in play on one world for part of a day (T4). A world turns it on with `terraform set TraceGasGatheringEnabled on`; a world that never recorded the switch, which includes every world played on 0.11.0, takes the config's off. **Owed before it leaves experimental: two full day cycles measured live** | - | `TraceGasGatheringEnabled` |
| T1 | The trace line is 0.001 mol per 8000 L cell, a hundred times the 0.00001 mol a cell deletes (0.11.1; 0.0001 in 0.11.0) | chosen (owner, 2026-09-26) | At 0.0001 a spill just over about 25 mol on a Vulcan of 250,000 cells got no help at all. 0.001 covers spills up to about 250 mol there and stays a tenth of the thinnest gas any shipped world starts with (**MEASURED** by dump: Vulcan's nitrogen, about 0.011 to 0.014 per cell; Mars's thinnest, pollutant, 0.058), so no shipped air is a trace | A gas a player cares about is gathered, or a spill is not | `TraceGasLine`, 0 to 0.01 |
| T2 | Speed at the trace line 200 (settable 1 to 2000), doubling for every factor of ten below the line, smoothly, up to 10 times the line speed (0.11.3; 0.11.2 had a fixed cap of 2,000 and at most 250, 0.11.1 100 and 1,000, 0.11.0 a flat 50) | chosen (owner, 2026-09-26, on live data) | A flat factor left the tail of a spill lingering: the draw falls with the share, so the last moles were the slowest. Doubling per decade (`(line / share)^log10 2`) grows slower than the share falls, so a cell's draw peaks at the line and no cap can raise it. **MEASURED** on Vulcan (0.11.1): the drain rose about 1.8 times per doubling of the factor (T4), and a trace of oxygen reached 985 against the old cap of 1,000. The cap is relative so that any base keeps about 3.3 decades of doubling; a fixed cap would have cancelled the doubling at a high base. Past it more factor only crowds the last of a trace into fewer cells, and the tick budget (T3) binds before it on any base of a few dozen exchanging cells | Clears slower or burns harder than wanted | `TraceGasGathering`, the speed at the trace line, 1 to 2000; the cap is always 10 times it |
| T3 | What the cells keep of the extra is at most 1 % of a trace a tick (0.11.1 on; 0.11.0 charged the whole extra, most of which goes straight back) | chosen | Stops a large base draining a whole trace in a few ticks. It binds once n x `t` x (factor - 1) > 0.01 x N (n exchanging cells, N cells in the planet). In the T4 case (N 250,000, `t` 0.204) that is a factor above 875 with 14 cells or 584 with 21, which at the 0.11.2 base the scaled factor reaches below about 1.9 or 7 mol | Below that it, not the factor, paces the drain | No |
| T4 | The 2026-09-26 Vulcan spill (about 24 mol nitrous oxide and 1.5 mol oxygen, 250,000 cells, 14 to 21 exchanging cells, `t` 0.204) | **MEASURED** in part (0.11.1, rule on); the model **overestimates** | **Live:** at factors near 200 to 400 nothing burned in Vulcan's 400 K night and nitrous oxide stayed flat; at a factor near 900 (base 200, line 0.01) the cells held enough to keep burning at 401 K, 0.044 to 0.049 mol/s at 17 to 13 mol, and the tail went from 0.32 mol to under 0.000001 in 18 minutes across the night (data/n2o-clear-2026-09-26.csv in the CheatEngineExpert wiki). By day, a factor of 204 (line 0.001, base 100) drained about 0.011 mol/s at about 23 mol, and a factor of 417 (line 0.01) about 0.018 mol/s: about 1.8 times per doubling. A burning cell burns 6 % (1,240 K) to 13 % (400 K) a tick of what it holds above 0.0003 mol and gives the rest back; on Vulcan a cold night stopped it at low factors, which the model left out. Mixing between neighbouring cells is also left out. Without the rule the fire would have lasted 5 to 8 hours of burning | The rule helps less, or more | Two full day cycles measured live (owed, T0) |
| T5 | Gas held by cells is not in the planet's readout | BOUNDED | A cell beside open ground that uses none of a trace settles at the factor times the planet's density of it: the line speed (200 by default) at the line, up to 10 times it far below. With n such cells that is factor x n / N of the planet's own share: on a planet of 250,000 cells, at the line, under 1 % below 12 cells and 16 times it at 20,000; far below the line ten times those. A smaller planet reaches those shares with fewer cells | `terraform` reads a trace lower than the planet plus cells hold. Gas above the line is never held back | - |
| T6 | A spill too thin to burn without the rule can burn with it | accepted (owner, 2026-09-26) | Without the rule a cell burns an oxidiser only when its draw, `t` x the per-cell share, reaches 0.00001 mol: on the Vulcan of T4, a planet holding 12 mol or more. With it, from about 0.006 to 0.02 mol up (the factor at its cap of 10 times the line speed, or held by the budget). Between there and 12 mol the unmodded game deletes the draw in every cell every tick without a flame; with the rule those cells burn until the trace is gone. From 12 mol up to the line (250 mol) both burn, and the rule ends it sooner. A steady leak burns either way, because the planet settles where it loses what is leaking in | A brief burn beside the base after a small spill | `TraceGasGatheringEnabled` |
| T7 | How hard a gathered trace can burn | BOUNDED | A cell is handed at most base x line x `t` of a trace a tick, and that is at the line: the factor grows slower than the share falls, so even at the cap, 10 times the base, a trace far below the line is handed less (at base 2000 the cap of 20,000 applies below about 5e-7 mol per cell, where a cell is handed at most 0.01 mol at `t` 1). So the worst is 0.2 mol at the default 200 and `t` 1, 0.04 at `t` 0.2; at the highest setting, 2000, 2 mol and 0.41. The 1 % tick budget (T3) caps what all cells together keep: at the line 2.5 mol a tick on a planet of 250,000 cells, which at base 2000 already binds with seven exchanging cells at `t` 0.2. Worst case, oxygen with methane (two to one, 286 kJ per mole of methane): 1.1 MJ a tick into one cell at base 2000 and `t` 1, 0.23 MJ at `t` 0.2; 0.11 MJ and 0.023 at the default. The cell's exchange moves its energy `t` of the way back toward the planet every tick, so the heat it carries above the planet settles near that tick's input divided by `t`, about 1.1 MJ over the planet at base 2000 either way: on a Vulcan cell holding 1.3 MJ, about 2.5 MJ, a quarter of the 10 MJ at which a thing catches fire from hot air alone (`Thing.ShouldIgnite`), and on the order of a thousand kelvin hotter while it burns. That is within what a burning room in the game already reaches, and every such cell is already alight; at the default the rise is about a tenth of that. The spill in T4 peaks near 2 kJ a tick at the default | A trace fire hot enough to matter by energy alone | `TraceGasGathering`, `TraceGasLine` |

## Known consequences of the game's own constants at small planet sizes

Not assumptions, but easy to forget: the phase-change rates are absolute (1000 mol per tick melt, 3000
freeze, cloud buckets of 100,000 L), so per outdoor cell they run 20 times faster at Standard size
and 100 times at Short. Rain and snow will be more frequent on a small planet. Unplayed.
