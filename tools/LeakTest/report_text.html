The planet's air burns: what the headless runs measured

Terraforming Reloaded 0.13.0. Every run on the dedicated test server (game build 0.2.6428.27798, no player connected). Labels: measured is read from a run; modelled is worked out by PatchCheck or tools/Balance. Raw logs: tools/LeakTest/results/ (not committed).

Executive summary

What ran. LiveCheck -PlanetBurn (5 cases, switch on and off: all pass), the standing regression (default, -SaveLoad, -Reset, -Model -World Vulcan2: all pass), a liveness census, and 41 runs on Vulcan2 at size 0.05: 50 to 20,000 mol of oxygen, a tank vent and a pipe burst, midday, dusk and night, oxygen plus nitrous oxide, each with the switch on and off; two controls with nothing released; a 3 M mol planet dump at night. All on the dedicated test server; no player.
  • The switch works where it is meant to. With it off, any oxygen leak by day, from 50 mol up, sets every live outdoor cell on the planet burning for as long as the oxygen lasts and breaks every powered vent at every station, 100 m away included. With it on, the planet burns the oxygen in 23 to 52 s (1,000 to 20,000 mol), and the far stations' vents lose 3.3% (200 to 5,000 mol) to 10-11.7% (20,000 mol). measured
  • Only live cells burn. With no player, the live outdoor air is the cells kept by powered vents, passive vents and batteries: one each. Every burn away from the leak was in such a cell; walls, frames, solar panels, tanks, pipes and cables were never damaged, except a cable 2 m from a 20,000 mol leak (65-68%). measured
  • Design problem: the one-tick lead. In the tick the oxygen reaches the planet, before the hold-back arms, the planet hands it to every live outdoor cell. The amount per cell is identical with the switch on and off (1.3e-4 mol a cell at 1,000 mol; 5.6e-4 at 5,000; 2.2e-3 at 20,000). By day those cells are above methane's ignition point and burn for 1 to 6 ticks. After that tick nothing more arrives: the hold-back does its job. By dusk and night the handed oxygen does not light and drains back. This is the case for arming the hold-back whenever the ignition gate is open. measured
  • 3 M mol dump at night: 99% burnt in 103 s, the fire line reads burning until 243 s; +1,362.7 K (modelled 1,274-1,280 K); fading with a half-life of about 61 minutes; no errors in the server log. measured

Which cells are simulated: what keeps outdoor air live

An outdoor cell only exists, mixes, exchanges with the planet and can burn while something creates it or keeps it live. Everything else is the planet's read-only air, which is never handed gas and never burns.

From the game's code

  • Created or re-activated by AtmosphericsController.CloneGlobalAtmosphere (AtmosphericsController.cs:289-312; the thread-safe form AtmosphericsManager.cs:755-769), which sets the cell Active. Its callers include: a powered, switched-on active vent every atmospheric tick (ActiveVent.cs:386-391, 383; unpowered or off it never asks); a passive vent with an open grid every tick (PassiveVent.cs:27-55, Pipe.cs:557); a bursting pipe at each of its grids (Pipe.cs:425-433); any powered device that turns power into heat outdoors above the Armstrong limit (Device.cs:1512-1516); batteries (Battery.cs:553); a breathing entity once (Entity.cs:1222-1225); a burning thing or structure every fire tick (DynamicThing.cs:2211, SmallGrid.cs:185, Structure.cs:2267); portables, generators, furnaces, burners, rocket engines and weather events (36 call sites in all).
  • Kept while Atmosphere.IsLive holds (Atmosphere.cs:1134-1158): a room; or open neighbours and not within a sixth of the neighbour threshold of the planet's air; or awaiting a structure event; or a structure in the cell that blocks gravity and not air (a frame). A cell that fails it goes Active, Passive, MarkedForRemoval over two processing passes (AtmosphericsManager.cs:180-205) and is removed, handing its gas to the planet, unless something re-activates it first.
  • Exchanging with the planet: a live cell with open ground beside it and no cell there relaxes toward the planet every tick (Atmosphere.cs:1728-1731), which is where the planet's air is handed out.
  • Spreading: a cell further from the planet's air than the threshold makes real cells for its empty neighbours (Atmosphere.cs:2021-2068): a leak's own cloud.

Measured, one source per station, no player measured

@@LIVE@@

A frame, a wall and a solar panel make no cell of their own; an unpowered vent makes none; a powered vent (either direction), a passive vent and a battery each keep exactly one. On a base with no player, the only outdoor air the planet ever hands anything to is those few vent and device cells. That is where a planet-wide hand-out can land, and the only place it can burn.

The one-tick lead measured

The hold-back is decided at the planet tick. Oxygen that reaches the planet during one tick's mixing can be handed to live cells in that same mixing, before the fire lights at the next tick. It lands in every live cell exchanging with the planet, wherever it is: a vent at the far station (100 m) gets its share exactly like one at 16 m.

At the leak site (stations at 16, 32 and 100 m are out of reach of the leak's own cloud, which reaches about 6 m)

@@LEADSITE@@

Read with LiveCheck case 1 (5,000 mol at 280 m from a 20-cell block of base cells, by day): 1 tick, 20 cells Inflamed, at least 2.9e-4 mol of oxygen burnt, at most 8.0e-5 mol in one cell, 166 J. With the switch off the same block burnt for about 1,200 ticks: 9 mol, 5.2 MJ. Case 5 at night: 8.5e-4 mol handed out, none burnt. measured

What the table shows: the first hand-out is the same with the switch on and off, run for run, to three figures. The switch only decides what happens next. Off, the cells go on receiving oxygen and burn until the run ends; on, they get that one tick's share, burn it in 1 to 6 ticks by day, and nothing more arrives. The vent in each cell takes 3.3% damage (200-1,000 mol), 6.7% (5,000) or 10-11.7% (20,000) from that one burn. Each leak would add that again at every vent on the planet.

Leak matrix measured

Site: a new creative Vulcan2 world at size 0.05, release point R at (1301, 301, 1). Stations on one bearing at 2, 4, 8, 16 and 32 m and a far station 100 m the other way. Each holds an active vent (powered from its own battery, inward, on a pipe stub), a 6,000 L in-line tank, a straight and a heavy cable, a straight and an insulated pipe, a solar panel, an iron wall and a steel frame; the tanks at 8 m and the far station hold 467 mol of oxygen. Gas release: the gas goes into a 6,000 L in-line tank piped to a passive vent at R. Burst: into a lone 10 L pipe at R, filled past its 60,795 kPa burst pressure (small releases are put in hot to get there). Every run starts from the same save; the server is restarted for each. Runs end once the fire has been out 240 ticks, or after 600 ticks with the switch off (the off state never goes quiet: the planet keeps handing oxygen out).

Reading "Burnt in outdoor cells". It is derived, not counted: released, less what the planet burnt (its burn share times its oxidiser, logged every second tick), less what is left at the end. The night oxygen-only runs with the switch on measure its error: no outdoor cell burnt in them (no burning cell anywhere, every tick), yet it shows 6 to 12%. Read the day figures with that margin. "Local fire" and "Damage" are counted directly.

What lights a cell

Secondary to liveness: a cell that is not live cannot burn, whatever sparks near it.

From the game's code

  • The gate (Atmosphere.TryCombust, Atmosphere.cs:2205-2221): a cell burns when it is Sparked, or forced, or GasMixture.IsAutoIgnition() (a fuel above 1 mol in the cell and the cell above the fuel's ignition point, 573.15 K for methane), and holds at least the smallest amount of fuel and of oxidiser. The spark is cleared every tick.
  • Fire spreads by spark, not by heat. A burning (Inflamed) cell sparks every cell it gives gas to while mixing (Atmosphere.cs:1788-1792); gas given to the planet goes into the planet instead (:1784-1786), where the mod's spark prefix picks it up. Heat moves too, but a neighbour lights from heat only through autoignition.
  • What sets Sparked on an outdoor world cell: a burning thing (DynamicThing.cs:2210-2220), a burning structure (Structure.cs:2267-2272), a burning small-grid piece (SmallGrid.cs:185-190), a powered wall heater outdoors (WallHeater.cs:76-82), rocket exhaust (RocketEngineBase.cs:488-493, 574-582), a welding torch (WeldingTorch.cs:232), a road flare (RoadFlare.cs:172), dynamite and grenades (Dynamite.cs:137, Grenade.cs:143), and an atmospheric event with a spark (AtmosphericEventInstance.cs:528). Everything else in the list sparks a device's internal or pipe atmosphere (generators, combustion machines, pipe igniter, suits, circuits), not the world cell.
  • Not sparkers: active and passive vents never set Sparked, and carry no fire into pipes; a breaking powered cable calls WorldManager.Spark (Cable.cs:168-171), which only emits particles and does nothing on a dedicated server (WorldManager.cs:662-670, IsBatchMode).

Measured

On Vulcan2 by day the planet's air in a live cell is above methane's ignition point and holds 27 mol of methane a cell, so any live cell handed oxidiser lights by heat, with no sparker. Every burn at a station in the matrix was in a live vent or battery cell; no wall, frame, solar panel, cable, tank or pipe was ever damaged. The code shows a breaking cable cannot spark the air on a server.

Regression on the new build

  • LiveCheck -PlanetBurn (all three switches off by default; the driver turns the fire on): 9 of 10 passed on the first pass, case 1 reproducing the lead exactly (166.4 J). Case 2 with the fire on failed by 0.57 % on hydrogen: the driver's baseline was read on the main thread and raced a cell's exchange. Fixed in the driver (read at the start of the planet tick) and rerun: every product exact (carbon dioxide +7,200, pollutant +2,700, steam +400, nitrogen +1,900, methane -2,700, hydrogen -300).
  • LiveCheck default run: passed.
  • PatchCheck: cases 1 to 20 hold; the three switches ship off; all 12 broken rules are caught (7 earlier, 3 of the armed hold-back, 2 of the trace hold).
  • No errors in the server log in any follow-up run.
